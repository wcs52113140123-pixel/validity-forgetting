Figure 1. The failure is real, and it is invisible where a practitioner would look.
(a) Schematic of the setting. A class-incremental stream of ten tasks; a conformal predictor promises that its prediction set contains the true label with probability at least 0.90. A deployed system can check that promise only on the task just learned (T10), where it holds; on earlier tasks it fails unseen. The gauge under each task sketches coverage against the 0.90 tick (illustrative; quantified in b–d), and the two example sets show that when coverage fails the set does not grow, it misses the true label (quantified in f). (b) Final coverage of every method on every dataset and backbone (56 configurations). Column pairs: V, ViT-B/16; D, DINOv2. Each bar is filled to the configuration's coverage; the vertical tick marks 0.90 and a black frame marks the 4 configurations that reach it, all prototype-based (NCM and iCaRL on CIFAR-100/ViT-B/16 and CUB-200/DINOv2). 52 of 56 configurations fall below the promise. (c) Accuracy (dashed) and coverage (solid) on all seen classes along the ten-task stream, one panel per method family, averaged over the eight dataset-backbone configurations; the area between the curves is shaded. After ten tasks accuracy is 0.752, 0.723 and 0.413 for the prototype, replay and gradient families, coverage 0.876, 0.646 and 0.360. (d) Per-task coverage of the final model, MedMix-26 with ViT-B/16; colour is coverage on a scale centred at 0.90 and dot area is the shortfall below it. EWC, LwF and Seq-FT cover exactly 0.000 on each of the first four tasks. (e) The deployment check: coverage on the task just learned and on earlier tasks for each of the 56 configurations, paired by a line. Every configuration lies between 0.885 and 0.965 on the task just learned, so all pass the only check available at deployment, while earlier-task coverage spans 0.078 to 0.906. (f) Prediction-set size against final coverage, 56 configurations, with marginal densities per family (log scale). Sets average 0.90 labels in the 24 configurations covering below 0.50 and 6.90 in the 20 covering at 0.85 or above, so they do not grow to signal the loss. Split conformal prediction with the LAC score, α = 0.10, five class orderings; dashed lines mark 0.90.

Figure 2. Accuracy is retained; validity is not, and averaging hides how much.
(a) Accuracy loss (open marker) and coverage loss (filled) of each of the 56 configurations relative to the best-accuracy method in the same configuration, sorted by coverage loss and grouped by family; coverage loss exceeds accuracy loss in 29 of 56 configurations. (b) Ratio of mean coverage loss to mean accuracy loss per method that trains a classifier head; half-violins are bootstrap distributions (4,000 resamples of configurations), the marker and bar the point estimate and its 95% interval: experience replay 3.56 (2.95–4.72), Seq-FT 1.40, LwF 1.37, EWC 1.29. These ratios of means differ from the per-configuration medians quoted in Section 3.2, which summarise the same configurations another way. (c) Distribution of per-task coverage of the final model, one ridge per method, pooled over the eight configurations; vertical ticks mark medians. (d) Number of tasks out of ten that meet 0.90, as fractions of configurations stacked by family, against the binomial distribution a valid predictor would produce at these calibration and test sizes (black). Configurations significantly below that null at p < 0.05: 0 of 24 prototype, 8 of 8 replay, 24 of 24 gradient. (e) Empirical cumulative distribution of per-task coverage by family; diamonds mark the mean. The share of tasks below 0.90 is 0.717, 0.900 and 0.921 for the prototype, replay and gradient families, although their means are 0.876, 0.646 and 0.360. (f) Flow from accuracy rank to validity rank within each configuration; ribbon width is the number of configurations making that transition. 50.0% of configurations keep their rank. (g) Spearman correlation between the accuracy ranking and the validity ranking per configuration, with bootstrap 95% intervals over methods; filled markers have p < 0.05: positive in all eight and significant in five, so the accuracy leaderboard largely predicts the validity leaderboard.

Figure 3. The mechanism is ranking degradation, not scale drift.
(a) Schematic of the two candidate mechanisms. Bars are class scores, the ring marks the true label and the dashed line is the threshold calibrated on the task just learned. Under scale drift every score falls but the ranking is kept: the set at that threshold is empty, and a recalibrated (dotted) threshold recovers the true label with a set of the same size. Under ranking loss a wrong class overtakes the true one: the set holds only the wrong label, and recalibration recovers the true label only by admitting the wrong one too, so the set must grow. (b) Coverage gap against ranking degradation, measured as the earlier-minus-current change in the area under the risk-coverage curve (ΔAURC), 56 configurations, with the least-squares fit, its 95% band and marginal densities: R² = 0.890. (c) Partition of the explained variance of the coverage gap: shared by ranking and scale drift 0.816, ranking only 0.074, scale only 0.003 (total 0.893). (d) Bootstrap distributions of the coefficients in the joint regression of the gap on both drivers: standardised coefficients, ranking 1.16 (95% interval 0.73 to 1.64); scale -0.22 (-0.70 to 0.27), an interval that includes zero. (e) AURC and ECE on all seen classes along the stream, one strip per family and metric; both grow, but (c) and (d) show that the growth in ECE carries no information that ranking has not already carried. (f) ΔAURC (left halves) and ΔECE (right halves) by method family. (g) Per-task coverage of the final model, CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/16: angle is the task, rings are methods (prototype innermost), colour is coverage on a scale centred at 0.90.


Figure 4. Reserving calibration slots in the replay memory restores validity, and per-class thresholds on those slots make it hold under shift.
(a) Replay-buffer conformal calibration (RBCC). The memory of every class is split into replay slots, used for rehearsal as usual, and reserved slots, drawn uniformly at random before training and never trained on. The trained model is calibrated on the reserved slots alone, with one threshold per class. Because the reserved slots are exchangeable with test data from the same class, the guarantee holds per class (Section 2.6), which calibrating on replayed exemplars (seen in training) or on the current task (blind to earlier classes) cannot provide (b, c). (b) Mean coverage gap of the 16 replay-memory configurations under the four calibration sets: the current task, replayed exemplars, reserved slots (RBCC) and all past data (an oracle that requires full storage). Densities, quartile bars and the individual configurations. (c) Paired comparison of current-task calibration with RBCC: the 16 configurations on the left, and on the right the bootstrap distribution of the mean paired difference, -0.127 (95% interval -0.206 to -0.061); the gap falls in 15 of 16 configurations. (d) Final accuracy against mean coverage gap as the number k of reserved slots per class grows (marker size), experience replay at fixed memory m = 20 per class, mean over the four benchmarks and two backbones. With reserved slots the gap stays between 0.014 and 0.020 for every k ≥ 1 while accuracy falls from 0.733 at k = 1 to 0.704 at k = 10; calibrated on the replayed exemplars, the same memory leaves a gap of 0.229–0.308. (e) Mean prediction-set size against reserved slots per class k for the three schemes, CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/16, with 95% intervals. Grey band: below k = 9 no per-class quantile exists and all classes fall back to the pooled threshold. Yellow band: for 9 ≤ k < 19 the per-class quantile is the largest reserved score and sets inflate to 3.70–4.30. At k = 40 the per-class rule needs 1.84 labels against 1.54 for the oracle. (f) Mean per-class coverage on a class-balanced test set by scheme and k; every scheme is valid here, as it should be. (g) Coverage on a test mixture of the oldest classes against the replay budget, at k = 20: the per-class rule holds above 0.90 throughout (mean 0.9067) while both single-threshold schemes sit below it (0.8806 for the oracle, 0.8838 for one threshold on the reserved slots); shading marks the difference. (h) Coverage lost when the test mixture is shifted to the oldest classes, by scheme, in two regimes at matched budget (20 replay and 20 reserved slots per class): density, mean and 95% interval over class orderings. Frozen ViT-B/16: +0.0144 for the oracle, +0.0142 for one threshold, -0.0069 per class. ResNet-18 trained end to end: +0.0254, +0.0248 and -0.0014. (i) Coverage as the share of earlier classes in the test mixture rises from balanced to 80% and 100%, replay budget 20: the per-class rule rises from 0.905 to 0.912 while the oracle falls from 0.902 to 0.888. Panels e–i: calibration slots withheld from training; α = 0.10; five class orderings.

Figure 5. The failure and the remedy survive end-to-end training, a second backbone and a second conformal score.
(a) What is varied, and what holds. Top: the two training regimes, a pretrained ViT-B/16 kept frozen with only a linear head trained, and a ResNet-18 trained from scratch end to end (padlock = frozen, circular arrow = trained). Middle: in both regimes coverage meets the 0.90 tick on the task just learned but not on earlier tasks under current-task calibration, and calibrating on the reserved slots brings final coverage back to it (0.904 with a frozen backbone, 0.897 end to end; gauges otherwise illustrative, measured values in c and d). Bottom: the further factors crossed with them, two backbones (e), two conformal scores (f) and four benchmarks (g). (b) Single-task accuracy of the end-to-end network against epochs per task. The shaded band lies within 0.015 of the ceiling (0.927 at 450 epochs); the 150 epochs used (dashed line) reach 0.922. (c) Coverage on the task just learned and on earlier tasks in both regimes, current-task calibration for every method. Every method passes the deployment check in both regimes (0.898–0.908 end to end), while earlier-task coverage falls to 0.000–0.324 end to end, against 0.365–0.744 with the frozen backbone. (d) Final coverage of the end-to-end setting under the four calibration sets, with 95% intervals: current task 0.195, replayed exemplars 0.018, reserved slots (RBCC) 0.897, all past data 0.889. (e) Agreement between backbones (Bland–Altman): DINOv2 minus ViT-B/16 final coverage against their mean, per method and dataset. Mean difference -0.050; shading marks the 95% limits of agreement (-0.275 to 0.174). (f) Final coverage against set size under LAC (open) and APS (filled), per method, CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/16. APS, which never returns an empty set, raises the coverage of the gradient methods from 0.50–0.73 to 0.74–0.84 but leaves them below 0.90, at roughly five times the set size. (g) Mean coverage gap per family on the four benchmarks; the prototype family stays near zero (0.011–0.093) and the ordering of families is the same on every benchmark. Panels b–d: CIFAR-100, ten tasks, five class orderings.
